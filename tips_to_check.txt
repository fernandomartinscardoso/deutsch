   - Modalpartikel denn: denn is a modal particle that softens or adds a friendly, slightly puzzled tone—think “well”, “you know”, or “actually” in English. It’s most common in questions (“Was machst du denn?” ≈ “What's that you’re
  doing, then?”) and in statements that invite explanation (“Warum hast du das denn gemacht?” ≈ “Why on earth did you do that?”). Unlike the conjunction denn (“because”), the particle is unstressed, never
  appears at the sentence start, and doesn’t affect word order.  Use it to sound conversational and mildly inquisitive rather than formal.
   - Other modal particles: German has a whole family of modal particles that add nuance without changing the literal meaning. Common ones include:
      - ja – “you know”, reassuring (e.g. Das ist ja klar.)
      - doch – contrast or encouragement (e.g. Mach das doch!)
      - mal – softens a request, “just” (e.g. Schau mal! )
      - schon – “already/indeed”, often to calm (e.g. Das ist schon in Ordnung.)
      - eben – “simply”, factual tone (e.g. Das ist eben so.)
      - halt – “just”, resigned (e.g. So ist das halt.)
      - bloß / nur – “only”, limiting (e.g. Du hast bloß …)
      - wohl – “probably/indeed”, tentative (e.g. Er kommt wohl spät.)
      - etwa – “perhaps/anyway”, often in questions (e.g. Was willst du etwa?)
      - eigentlich – “actually”, mild correction (e.g. Ich wollte eigentlich …)
     These particles are unstressed, appear inside the clause, and give the speaker a conversational, often subtle emotional shade.
